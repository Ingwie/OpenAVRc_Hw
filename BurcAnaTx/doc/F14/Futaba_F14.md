# Futaba F14
Here is the interior of a Futaba F14 radio transmitter.  
However, all Futaba transmitters with the same form factor as the F14 such as the FC14, FC15, FC18, and FC28 can be used for this project.  
You can see the wiring for the two proportional sticks and the coder board.  
This radio controller also features proportional inputs for additional potentiometers or switches.  
We will use one of the 7 inputs on the encoder board. It is best to use channels 5 through 7 to keep the stick channels functional.

## Derivation of the CH3 channel
![](https://github.com/Ingwie/OpenAVRc_Hw/blob/V3/BurcAnaTx/doc/F14/xxxx.jpg)  

The radio's microprocessor operates at 5V, whereas the Mini Nano requires 5V.  
This Arduino features a Vin input that supports up to approximately 12V and generates its own 5V supply.  
The transmitter is powered by a 2S LiPo battery (7.4V), which will also be used to supply power to the Nano's Vin pin.  
Naturally, the connection must be made after the main switch.  

## CPPM Synchronization
To optimize the operation of the BurcAnaTx module, a CPPM signal generated by the transmitter (where available) can be fed into it.  
This signal then serves as a synchronization reference for the BurcAnaTx module.  
The BurcAnaTx encoder can repeat its signal up to six times to optimize the quality of the signal received by the receiver.  
Without CPPM synchronization, the module must be configured for five repetitions, which slightly slows down message reception.  
With synchronization enabled, the number of repetitions can be reduced or even eliminated entirely.  
In the case of the F14, the PPM signal is available.
